# PB1 · Run a weekly Copilot tips programme

**Who this is for:** whoever has been handed "drive Copilot adoption" and a list of licences.

**What it is:** a two-touch weekly rhythm. On **Monday**, send three tips and a challenge. On **Friday**, post an open check-in. It costs about an hour a week to run, and it beats a one-off training session because it keeps showing up.

**Last verified:** pending re-test

---

## Why it works

Training days teach features. Adoption comes from habit, and habit needs a reason to try something *this week*, with a real piece of work, plus somewhere to say whether it worked. The Monday email provides the first. The Friday check-in provides the second, and it's also your best source of honest feedback.

## The weekly rhythm

### Monday: Hot Tips email

**Structure (keep it identical every week, because predictability is the point):**

1. **Subject:** `Copilot Hot Tips: [the most useful tip, as a verb phrase]`, e.g. *"Copilot Hot Tips: tell your inbox what to do"*.
2. **One-line intro:** what's in it and who it's for. No "exciting news".
3. **Three tips**, each with:
   - a plain-English heading that says what it does (*"Ask the site, not the file"*)
   - two sentences on what it is
   - **Try it this week:** one copy-paste prompt, written for *your* organisation's real work
   - the platform (Windows, web, Mac) and a link to Microsoft's own page
4. **Tip 3 is always "for the tinkerers":** something more advanced (Copilot Studio, agents, automation) for the 10% who want more. The other 90% skip it happily.
5. **This week's challenge:** one small thing to try, plus "tell us on Friday".
6. **A footer** stating that features need a licence and roll out in stages, so they may not see everything yet.

**Rules that mattered:**
- **Every prompt uses your organisation's nouns.** "Summarise this document" gets ignored. "Find every revision of the traffic management plan and tell me which is current" gets tried.
- **Lead with what's new.** Source tips from Microsoft's official release notes and support pages. When the release notes go quiet (they do, sometimes for weeks), use freshly published support pages instead.
- **Never repeat a tip.** Keep a tips log (date, feature, source link) and check it before each issue.
- **Be honest about caveats in a private note to yourself** before you send: licence needed, phased rollout, preview, admin opt-in, cost. Then put the one caveat users actually need into the tip itself.
- **Flag governance-sensitive features** (recording people, agents that act on your behalf, anything that bills on usage) with a "talk to IT first" line.

### Friday: open check-in

Post this in the team channel, not by email. Replies are public, which is the point.

**Structure:**
1. **Title:** `Copilot Friday check-in: [the challenge, as a question]`, e.g. *"Did Copilot get it right first go?"*
2. A one-line reminder of Monday's challenge.
3. **One open question** that's safe to answer badly: *"What did Copilot turn up that you'd have struggled to find yourself?"* or *"Did it beat the scroll?"*
4. **One quick win** for people who didn't try Monday's tip: a 30-second version.
5. An explicit invitation to share failures: *"If it got it wrong, tell us how. That's just as useful."*

## What we learned from feedback

<!-- MAINTAINER: drafted from the tips format. Replace or confirm against the real feedback notes before v0.1. -->

- **People wanted fewer tips, not more.** Three per week is the ceiling. Two is fine.
- **"Try it this week" prompts got used; feature descriptions didn't.** If a tip has no copy-paste prompt, cut it.
- **Friday replies were mostly failures,** and those were the most valuable signal. They told us which features weren't rolled out yet, which prompts were too vague, and where licences were missing.
- **Licence confusion was the #1 question.** State on every tip whether it needs the paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licence.
- **The tinkerer tip created champions.** The few people who built something in Copilot Studio became the go-to helpers in their teams.

## Setting it up

1. **Week 0:** agree the sender (a team name works better than a person), the channel for Friday posts, and who checks the governance caveats.
2. **Build the tips log:** a simple list with date, tip, feature, source URL, and a "used" flag.
3. **Draft on the weekend, send Monday morning.** Drafting can itself be a scheduled Copilot (or other AI) task: research the release notes, check the log, draft both the email and the private caveats note.
4. **Every Friday,** copy the best reply into the tips log as a note. Those notes become next quarter's training content.
5. **Monthly:** re-check that last month's tips still work. Features move.
